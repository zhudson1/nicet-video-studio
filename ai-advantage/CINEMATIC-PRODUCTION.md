# Cinematic Production Standard — The AI Advantage
All ten episodes are **production briefs**, not approved or rendered final videos. Do not auto-publish.

## Visual style
9:16, target 1080x1920, 24 or 30 fps; realistic commercial cinematography, natural hand anatomy, real reflections, credible physical movement, deep charcoal and mint highlights. Avoid fake branded UI, fake testimonials, or unsupported claims. Use original screen recordings for any actual software UI.

## Narrative format
0–3 sec: disruptive visual hook + one clear claim/question.
3–12 sec: relatable problem illustrated with an original scene.
12–40 sec: accurate demonstration with on-screen evidence, alternating macro detail and wide composition.
40–55 sec: practical takeaway and satisfying visual payoff.
55–65 sec: single CTA (follow, save, or free toolkit).
Use a 65–90 sec version only where original educational depth supports it.

## Editing rules
Cut on meaning, not randomly. Vary shots every 2–5 sec when useful. Captions: 2–5 words per beat, high contrast, mobile-safe margins, never covering actual UI controls. Natural conversational narration. Restrained sound design, music licensed for commercial social use. Loudness normalized, no clipping. Verify claims and captions manually.

## Ten scene concepts
01 — Late-night overwhelmed entrepreneur, rapidly multiplying tasks, then three authentic screen-recorded tool demos, clean desk payoff.
02 — Inbox avalanche; typing the same email repeatedly; real prompt-to-draft transformation with human review.
03 — Chaotic meeting whiteboard; five distinct deliverables emerge from an authorized transcript; verify actions against source.
04 — Three business concept prototypes visualized in cinematic real-world settings; emphasize customer validation over passive-income hype.
05 — Repetitive copy/paste loop; visual flowchart becomes a real automation; show test and failure handling.
06 — Two AI assistants side by side; identical prompt, comparison scorecard; record actual results and date.
07 — Creator editing timeline; permissioned long video split into distinct original commentary clips; avoid mere reposting.
08 — Small-business owner juggling tasks; five specific prompts demonstrated with fictional sample data.
09 — Laptop with open-source tools; actual local render and workflow logs; disclose usage and infrastructure limits.
10 — Before/after desk routine; stopwatch and documented quality checks; no invented measured savings.

## Asset requirements for each episode
- 4–8 original cinematic shots or licensed footage, rights logged
- 2–4 genuine software UI captures when discussing a tool
- Reviewed script and voiceover
- Caption transcript, attribution ledger, platform caption, thumbnail
- Final human approval before social publishing

## Production stages
script -> fact check -> shot list -> footage rights -> cinematic assets -> genuine UI capture -> edit/voice/captions -> QC -> human approval -> publish -> analytics.

## Zero-subscription constraint
Keep GitHub Actions and FFmpeg as orchestration/render foundation, but hyperrealistic generative video is NOT guaranteed free. Prefer original smartphone photography, practical macro filming, free licensed footage and locally rendered 3D/motion graphics. Use paid generative models only with explicit approval and known credits/cost. Do not call an untested slideshow a finished cinematic video.
