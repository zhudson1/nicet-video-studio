## Fact-checked conceptual script
“Ever wonder what actually happens when a fire alarm activates? First, an initiating device, such as a smoke detector or manual pull station, reports an alarm condition to the fire alarm control unit. The control unit processes the signal according to its programming. It can activate notification appliances like horns, strobes, or speakers, alerting occupants. Depending on the system design, it may also command connected building functions and transmit a signal to a supervising station. The panel also monitors circuits and devices for certain faults. That’s the basic sequence: detect, process, notify, and respond. Follow H Fire Safety for more fire alarm lessons.”

## Accuracy notes
This is a high-level educational explanation of a typical system, not a universal sequence of operation. Detector sensing, signaling, notification, off-premises transmission and auxiliary control depend on system type, approved design and programming. Do not suggest all systems have all features or that a detector alone always activates every notification appliance.
