## Episode 02 — SLC basics
**Narration:** "The signaling line circuit—or SLC—is how a compatible addressable fire alarm panel communicates with its field devices. Detectors and modules have programmed addresses, allowing the panel to identify points individually. The circuit's wiring method, capacity, and fault behavior depend on the panel and devices. And here's the key: an SLC is not automatically a notification appliance circuit. Always check the manufacturer's installation instructions and approved plans."
**Visuals:** panel hero; conceptual data pulse; addressable device close-ups; SLC versus NAC split screen; documentation CTA.

## Episode 03 — Class A versus Class B
**Narration:** "Class A and Class B describe different circuit pathway performance characteristics—not simply whether a cable returns to the panel. A Class B pathway generally doesn't provide a redundant path around a single open. A properly designed Class A pathway can maintain operation beyond a single open, depending on the system and fault location. Isolation, shorts, and specific performance requirements need separate consideration. Never assume a loop is Class A just because you see return conductors."
**Visuals:** conceptual paths with labels; one open fault; no invented panel wiring; approved design CTA.

## Episode 04 — EOL supervision
**Narration:** "An end-of-line resistor helps certain supervised fire alarm circuits distinguish normal wiring from an open or short. The resistor's value and placement are not universal: they depend on the listed equipment and circuit design. Some systems use different supervision methods, including Class A pathways. Don't guess the resistor value—use the manufacturer's instructions and approved drawings."
**Visuals:** conceptual monitored circuit; open and short animations; resistor placement caveat; manual CTA.
